Air compressor maintenance schedule

For shop compressors, both piston (reciprocating) and rotary screw. The daily tank drain is the one everybody skips and the one that rusts tanks out from the inside.

Download the PDF

Typical intervals from manufacturer manuals and published service schedules. Your machine's manual comes first.

Daily (both kinds)

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Drain water from the tankDailyLet pressure down first, then open the drain slowly
Drain line traps and drop legsDaily
Check oil level (piston) or coolant level (screw)DailyMilky oil means water is getting in
Look it over, tighten loose fittingsDaily

Weekly

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Pull the safety (pressure relief) valve by handWeeklyMake sure it pops and reseats
Check the intake air filterWeeklyReplace when dirty
Clean cooling fins and the intercoolerWeeklyPiston units
Listen for air leaks in the unit and linesWeekly

Piston compressors: oil and belts

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
First oil change (break-in)100 hours or 1 monthNew units
Oil changeEvery 500 hours or 3 monthsUp to 1,000 hours or 6 months on synthetic, per maker
Belt tension and conditionMonthly
Inspect components, lubricate motorYearly (about 2,000 hours)

Rotary screw: by hours

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Coolant filterFirst at 150 hours, then every 2,000 hours or yearly
Coolant / compressor fluidEvery 2,000 to 8,000 hoursDepends heavily on the fluid; check your manual
Air filterAbout 4,000 hoursSooner if its pressure-drop indicator trips
Clean the cooler coresAbout 4,000 hoursSooner if it's running hot
Separator elementWhen pressure drop says soSome makers cap it at about 9,000 hours or 3 years
Safety valve test by a technicianYearly

Required by code? The tank is a pressure vessel, and some states require periodic pressure-vessel inspections. Code items apply where your city, state or insurer has adopted them. Requirements vary by jurisdiction, so check local code.

What changes these intervals

A hot or dusty room, humidity (more water to drain) and how hard it runs. Every manual says to shorten intervals in dirty conditions.
