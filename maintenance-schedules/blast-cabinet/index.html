Blast cabinet and blast pot maintenance schedule

For abrasive blast cabinets and portable pressure blast pots. Worn blast hose can burst under pressure, so squeeze it for soft spots and replace it before the wear reaches the fabric.

Download the PDF

Typical intervals from manufacturer manuals and published service schedules. Your machine's manual comes first.

Daily, before blasting (air off)

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Blast hose: squeeze it for soft spotsDailySoft spots mean it's worn; replace it
Couplings tight, lock pins and safety cables onDaily
Nozzle washer in place, threads not wornEvery time the nozzle goes onA loose nozzle can blow out under pressure
Deadman handle worksDailyOnly engages with the safety lock down, springs back up when let go
Pot: empty the abrasive trap screenAt least twice a day
Cabinet: media level, debris screen, door gasketDaily
Drain the air filter and moisture separatorDailyWet air makes media clump and stop flowing
Cabinet: dust bin, empty by half fullDaily

Daily, while blasting

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Leaks at couplings, pop-up valve, door, pipingDailyStop and fix; abrasive cuts through a leak fast
Dust at the cabinet exhaust or door sealsDailyMeans a leaking or loaded filter cartridge
Drain the collector's pulse reservoirEnd of each day

Weekly

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Nozzle: replace when worn 1/16 inch over sizeWeeklyOr if the liner is cracked
Gloves and window cover lensWeeklyExtra static shocks can be the first sign of worn gloves
Outlet valve diaphragm and flex hosesWeekly
Collector's inline filterWeekly

Monthly

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Pop-up valve and sealMonthlyReplace at the first crack, groove or dry rot
Reclaimer wear plate and linersMonthlyReplace when worn through to metal
Pot exhaust mufflerMonthlySlow depressurizing means it's clogging

Required by code? OSHA 1910.94 covers abrasive blasting: the nozzle must be bonded and grounded against static, blast enclosure baffles inspected regularly and replaced when needed, dust leaks repaired promptly, exhaust air run through a dust collector, and operators in blast rooms must wear NIOSH-approved abrasive blasting respirators. Code items apply where your city, state or insurer has adopted them. Requirements vary by jurisdiction, so check local code.

What changes these intervals

The media you run (hard, sharp media wears nozzles, hoses and liners much faster), how dirty the parts are, and how fast the media breaks down.
