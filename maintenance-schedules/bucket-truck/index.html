Bucket truck maintenance schedule

For bucket trucks and aerial lift trucks used by tree crews. There are really two machines here: the aerial device, which has its own inspection rules, and the truck underneath it, which gets a severe-duty schedule because the boom runs on engine hours with no miles.

Based on OSHA's aerial lift rules, the ANSI/SAIA A92.2 standard and manufacturer guidance. Your lift's manual and your employer's or utility's rules come first.

Required by code? OSHA requires the daily control test, fall protection, the setup rules and the boom-stowed-before-driving rule. The yearly periodic inspection and dielectric test come from the ANSI/SAIA A92.2 standard, which is voluntary unless your employer, insurer or a utility contract requires it, and many line-clearance contracts do, often more often than yearly. An insulated boom that has gone more than 12 months without a dielectric test shouldn't be trusted as insulated. Recent editions of the standard also call for a major structural inspection after about 15 years in service. A non-insulated boom must never be treated as insulated near power lines.

What changes these intervals

Hours of use, how hard it works, and line-clearance work near energized lines, which usually comes with shorter test cycles written into the contract.
