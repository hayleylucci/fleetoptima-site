Core aerator maintenance schedule

For walk-behind and stand-on core aerators. Tines and drive chains take the beating, so most of the work is daily and every 25 hours. A season of aerating can put a lot of hours on in a few weeks.

Download the PDF

Typical intervals from manufacturer manuals and published service schedules. Your machine's manual comes first.

Break-in (new aerators)

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
First engine oil changeAfter 5 hoursSome models change the oil filter too

Every day

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Check engine oilDaily
Tine drive chains: tension, condition, lubeDaily to every 25 hoursDirty chains need it daily
Check the tinesDailyReplace bent or worn ones; never straighten them
Look for loose fastenersDaily
Clean grass and dirt off the machine and engineDaily
Test the safety switchesDailyStand-on models

By hours

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Grease tine shaft bearings and idlersEvery 25 to 50 hours
Belt tension and conditionEvery 25 to 50 hours
Foam pre-cleanerEvery 25 hours on walk-behindsMore often in dust or sand
Tine reel nut torqueEvery 50 hoursPer your manual's torque spec
Tire pressure, spark arresterEvery 50 hours
Engine oilEvery 50 to 100 hours
Oil filterEvery 100 hours
Hydraulic fluid and filtersEvery 100 to 250 hoursDepends on the model
Fuel filterEvery 250 to 800 hours
Paper air filterEvery 300 to 500 hoursSooner in dust

Yearly or before storage

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Grease caster pivots, hubs and idler pivotsYearlyStand-on models
Re-torque wheel lug and hub nutsYearly
Coat the tines with rust preventiveBefore storage
Lubricate chains and bearings after pressure washingEvery wash
Check the batteryMonthly in season

What changes these intervals

Dusty or sandy soil (air filter and chains) and rocky or hard ground (tine wear and reel torque).
