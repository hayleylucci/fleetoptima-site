Daily truck and trailer inspection checklist

The walk-around for a crew truck pulling an equipment trailer, before you leave the yard. It takes about five minutes, and it's the checklist most likely to stop a wheel from coming off on the highway.

TaskHow oftenNotes✓

Review the last inspection report if it listed problemsBefore driving

Required by code? Federal rules (FMCSA) treat a truck as a commercial motor vehicle when the truck or truck-and-trailer is rated 10,001 lb or more and it's used in interstate commerce. Most 1-ton trucks pulling an equipment trailer are over that weight. For those, the driver must be satisfied the rig is safe before driving, a written report is needed when a problem is found, and each truck and trailer needs a yearly inspection with proof on board. Equipment over 10,000 lb needs at least 4 tie-downs. Trucks used only within one state follow that state's rules: Alabama, for example, applies the federal rules to intrastate trucks of 26,001 lb or more. Requirements vary by state, so check with yours.

What changes these intervals

Whether your rig counts as a commercial vehicle, where you drive it, and how heavy the trailer is.
