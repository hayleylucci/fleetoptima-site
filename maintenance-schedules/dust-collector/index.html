Dust collector maintenance schedule

For cartridge and baghouse dust collectors. The pressure gauge is the whole story: log it daily, and a steady climb tells you the filters are loading up or the pulse cleaning has quit.

Typical intervals from manufacturer manuals and published service schedules. Your machine's manual comes first.

Daily

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Read and log the pressure dropDailyWatch the trend, not just the number
Pulse cleaning worksDailyValves fire, timer set, air pressure right, no air line leaks
Drain water from the pulse air supplyDailyWet or oily air cakes dust on the filters
Check the dust bin or drum, empty itDailyDrums: empty by 2/3 full
Look for dust at the exhaustDailyDust coming out means a filter problem

By the gauge and yearly

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Replace filtersWhen pressure stays high after cleaningOften 3 to 6 months in heavy dust, about yearly in light duty
Compressed-air filters on the pulse systemPer your manual
Full filter and module inspectionYearlyAbrasion, tears, heavy buildup
Drum lid clamps and gasketsYearly
Explosion vents and isolation dampersYearly at leastUnblocked, undamaged; replace damaged parts right away
Dust hazard analysis (DHA) currentReview at least every 5 yearsAnd after any change to materials or process

Required by code? NFPA 660 (2025 edition) pulls NFPA 652, 654, 664 and the other combustible dust standards into one document. Facilities handling combustible dust need a dust hazard analysis (DHA), revalidated at least every 5 years and after process changes, and housekeeping is reviewed as part of it. If your collector has explosion vents, isolation valves or suppression, those carry inspection requirements of their own. Code items apply where your city, state or insurer has adopted them. Requirements vary by jurisdiction, so check local code.

What changes these intervals

How much dust goes through it and how clean the pulse air is. Heavy dust can mean new filters every few months instead of once a year.
