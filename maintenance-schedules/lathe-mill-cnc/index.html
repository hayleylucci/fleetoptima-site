Lathe, mill and CNC machine maintenance schedule

For manual lathes and mills and CNC machining centers. Coolant is the part that gets skipped: a refractometer reading takes a minute and tells you whether the mix still matches what the coolant maker calls for.

Typical intervals from manufacturer manuals and published service schedules. Your machine's manual comes first.

Daily / every shift

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Way lube tank full, pump putting out oilDailyUse the way oil your manual names
Coolant levelEach shiftMore often with through-spindle coolant
Coolant concentration with a refractometerDailyMatch the coolant maker's range
Clear chips from way covers, pan and chip screenEnd of each shift
Drain water from the air line and filterDaily
Guards in place and workingDaily
Manual machines: oil the ways, ball oilers, leadscrewDaily, before runningCheck headstock oil level too

Weekly

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Skim tramp oil off the coolantWeeklyRemove any oil floating on top
Clean chip basket and coolant collectorWeekly
Grease the chuck at each fittingDaily to weeklyPer your machine

Monthly

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Gearbox oil levelMonthlyCNC spindles with a gearbox
Inspect way covers, light oil if neededMonthly
Belts: wear and tensionMonthly
Lube lines reaching the ways and ballscrewsMonthlyTrained person

Every 6 months and yearly

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Dump the coolant, clean the tank, refillEvery 6 monthsSooner if it won't hold concentration
Hoses and lube lines for cracksEvery 6 months
Check backlash and taperYearlyMachines settle, and that shifts geometry
Change gearbox oilYearlyManual lathe headstocks: per manual, often by hours
Clean the way lube pump intake screenYearly
Hydraulic oil (if equipped)Yearly

What changes these intervals

Hours, number of shifts, and how much way and hydraulic oil ends up in the coolant. Heavier use means more chips to clear and coolant that needs checking more often.
