Utility and equipment trailer maintenance schedule

For utility and equipment trailers with electric brakes. Equipment trailers live near their weight limit and do lots of short heavy hauls, which is exactly what wears out bearings and brakes.

Download the PDF

Typical intervals from manufacturer manuals and published service schedules. Your machine's manual comes first.

Every trip

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Test the brakesEvery use
Check the breakaway battery and switchEvery use
Check tire pressureBefore each trip
Re-torque lug nuts after a wheel is mountedAfter the first few dozen milesCommon practice is about 10, 25 and 50 miles

Weekly

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Check tires for cuts, wear and bulgesWeekly

Every 3 months or 3,000 miles

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Adjust brakes3 months / 3,000 milesNot needed on self-adjusting brakes
Grease suspension bolts and equalizers3 months / 3,000 miles
Torque lug nuts3 months / 3,000 miles

Every 6 months or 6,000 miles

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Check brake magnets6 months / 6,000 miles
Check the brake controller6 months / 6,000 miles
Check suspension parts and wheels6 months / 6,000 milesCracks, wear, looseness

Yearly or 12,000 miles

TaskHow oftenNotes✓
Clean and repack wheel bearings12 months / 12,000 milesEasy-lube hubs still need this yearly
Replace seals if removed, check for leaks12 months / 12,000 miles
Inspect brake linings, drums and wiring12 months / 12,000 miles
Inspect springs and hanger welds12 months / 12,000 miles

What changes these intervals

Loads near the weight limit, lots of short heavy hauls, and wet or salty roads (bearings and brakes).
